About the Project

Detailed overview of research objectives, dataset architecture, data preparation methodology, visualization techniques, and analytical limitations.

1. Project Objectives

The primary purpose of Global Fuel Prices and Taxation Trends is to investigate the complex economic and fiscal mechanisms that drive retail fuel prices worldwide. Specific objectives include:

Disentangle Price Components

Isolate global baseline commodity price movements (Brent crude) from domestic fuel excise duties, VAT, and fiscal levies.

Regional & Geopolitical Disparities

Evaluate spatial variations across geographic regions to understand why neighboring countries experience drastically differing pump costs.

Subsidy Policy Impact

Determine how different subsidy levels protect consumers or create long-term fiscal liabilities for governments during energy crises.

Income Level Equity

Examine whether higher national income levels correlate with more stringent carbon taxation and higher retail fuel prices.

3. Data Preparation & Cleansing Methodology

To ensure analytical accuracy and cross-country comparability, a rigorous data preprocessing pipeline was implemented:

Step 1
Currency & Unit Standardization

All local currency pump prices were converted to United States Dollars per Liter (USD/Liter) using official historical exchange rates to eliminate exchange distortions.

Step 2
Missing Value Handling

Countries with erratic or discontinuous time series were audited. Missing intermittent observations were handled using linear temporal interpolation where valid.

Step 3
Categorical Harmonization

Nations were mapped to standardized World Bank income levels (High, Upper Middle, Lower Middle, Low) and assigned normalized subsidy tiers for consistent aggregation.

4. Tableau Visualizations Created

6. Analytical Limitations

  • Exchange Rate Volatility: Converting local currency retail prices into USD creates artificial fluctuations during periods of severe currency devaluation or rapid inflation.
  • Sub-National Tax Divergence: Certain large federal countries (e.g., United States, India, Canada) levy significant state or provincial fuel taxes, which are represented as national weighted averages in high-level datasets.
  • Reporting Lag: Developing economies may report retail prices with varying lag times compared to the instantaneous spot trading of Brent Crude oil.
  • Informal Fuel Markets: Official dataset prices represent formal regulated retail pump prices and do not account for parallel or black-market fuel transactions in crisis-affected areas.
